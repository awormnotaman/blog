> [!abstract] Setting
> It's sometime between 587 and 539 BC.
>
> We are all Israelite children in Babylon during the exile. We don't remember our homeland, our parents do, but they are so busy working on excavating that new canal that they can't tell us much of what it was like.
>
> Our clothes are a little ragged, mostly fine, but definitely raggedy.
>
> We are a little hungry most days, but that is fine, we know that our people are doing their best to care for us. Besides, our parents say our Yahweh will redeem us anyway.

> [!NOTE] Narrator
> It was a normal Rishon (that is Sunday). The first day of the week. But here we are, all of us sprinting headlong down the dusty side street of Tel Abib on the River Kebar.
>
> We go past one, two, three houses, we can smell the dust in the air from our feet thudding on the hard packed soil of the street. We take right skidding around the corner, past one, two, three houses.
>
> BANG! The door of the fourth house slams open as we all come tumbling in, ass over tea kettle. Sweating, and panting, completely out of breath. The young boy at the front with scraggly hair yells out, "Sah-Bah," (that is Grandpa).
>
> Into the room from the back door comes hobbling the ancient man we all know and love dearly, he always listens to our problems. With one incredibly bushy grey eyebrow raised, the ancient one, with a note of concern, "What is it Banim?" (that is children).
>
> This is where the story comes tumbling out of us.

> [!Info] Child 01
> "Sah-Bah, we got cornered by some Babylonian teenagers who were so mean."

> [!warning] Child 02
> "We were stuck in an alleyway so that we couldn't escape."

> [!success] Child 03
> "They said that their elohim (elo-hee-im), that he made everything."

> [!help] Grandfather
> "Oy vey…" says Grandfather as he sits down on a stool with a crackle pop of his knees, "Why don't you tell me the full story they told you?"

> [!NOTE] Narrator
> We all start at once, the words spilling out fast and tangled, the way they do when something has got under your skin and you can't shake it loose.

> [!Info] Child 01
> "They said — they said at the very, very beginning—"

> [!warning] Child 02
> "—before *anything*, Sah-Bah, there was no sky and no dry ground—"

> [!success] Child 03
> "—and nothing had a name yet. That's how *they* tell it."

> [!Info] Child 01
> "Just water. Two big waters all churned up together."

> [!help] Grandfather
> Sah-Bah's eyebrow goes up, slow, like it always does. "Water, you say. With no shape, and no name. The deep." He says a word soft, almost to himself. "*Tehom.*" (that is the deep). "Go on, go on."

> [!warning] Child 02
> "They had names for the waters! The sweet one was Apsu (Ap-soo), and the big salty sea was Tiamat (Tee-ah-maht), and she was like the mother of everybody."

> [!success] Child 03
> "And out of the waters the gods got born, Sah-Bah, lots and lots of them. But — and this is the silly part — the little gods were so *loud*, all stamping and shouting, that old Apsu couldn't sleep, so he decided he'd just destroy all his own children to get some quiet!"

> [!Info] Child 01
> "His own children!" *(scandalised)*

> [!warning] Child 02
> "But a clever god named Ea (Ay-ah) found out first," *(lowering his voice all dramatic)*, "and he sang a sleepy spell and put Apsu to sleep and… that was the end of Apsu."

> [!NOTE] Narrator
> Sah-Bah just nods, slow.

> [!success] Child 03
> "And *that's* when their big hero got born," *(and there's no wonder in it, only a kind of worry)*. "Marduk (Mar-dook). They said he was the biggest and the shiniest god, like he was made of sunlight, Sah-Bah, with fire when he opened his mouth. They kept *saying* it, over and over, like they wanted us to be afraid of him. And his grandfather gave him the four winds to play with like toys, and the winds made big waves all over the sea."

> [!Info] Child 01
> "And the waves bothered Tiamat, the sea-mother, and the other gods kept whispering, 'He killed your husband, are you just going to sit there?' So she got *so* angry. She made an army. Eleven *monsters*, Sah-Bah—"

> [!warning] Child 02
> "—dragons! and snakes! and scorpion-men with poison for blood!" *(and he shivers, because the big boys had made it all sound so real)*

> [!success] Child 03
> "—and she picked a champion called Qingu (Kin-goo) to lead them, and she gave him a magic Tablet that decides everyone's fate, and she made him the king."

> [!help] Grandfather
> Sah-Bah folds his hands. "And these monsters frightened the other gods, I suppose."

> [!Info] Child 01
> "They were *so* scared! They asked the strongest gods to go fight Tiamat, and every single one of them looked at her and ran away! Nobody was brave enough."

> [!warning] Child 02
> "So they all went to young Marduk. And Sah-Bah, listen to what he said. He said, 'I will fight her. But if I win, you all have to make me King. Number one, forever. And whatever I say just *happens* — and nobody can ever change my word.'"

> [!success] Child 03
> "And they tested him," *(quieter now)*. "They pointed at a picture of stars and Marduk just *said a word* and the stars vanished. And he said another word and they came back. And the big boys looked right at us when they said it, Sah-Bah. Like — like *only* their god can do that. Like ours can't."

> [!Info] Child 01
> *(very quiet, and then it comes out small and afraid)* "But Sah-Bah… Abba *says* Yahweh doesn't have to fight anybody. That He just speaks, and a thing *is*. But… what if Abba's wrong? What if their Marduk really did make all of it, and we've had it backwards our whole lives? What if our God is just… *our* story, and theirs is the true one?"

> [!NOTE] Narrator
> The other children go still. It is the question none of us wanted to say out loud, and now it is sitting in the middle of the room, and we can't take it back.

> [!help] Grandfather
> The bushy eyebrow softens, but Sah-Bah does not rush. "Keep going," he says, gently. "Tell me all of it first. Then we will weigh it together, you and I."

> [!warning] Child 02
> "Well, then there was the *battle*," *(and his voice drops low, because this was the part that frightened us most)*. "Marduk took his bow and his thunderbolts and all the winds, and he went after Tiamat. She opened her giant mouth to swallow him—"

> [!success] Child 03
> "—but he shoved a wind right in so she couldn't shut it!"

> [!Info] Child 01
> "—and then he shot an arrow and… he beat the sea." *(almost a whisper)*

> [!warning] Child 02
> "And then, Sah-Bah," *(and he does the next part with his hands)*, "he took Tiamat's huge body and he split it in two. Like splitting a fish to dry it. And with the top half he made the *sky*, way up high, and—" *(he stops, because this is the strange part)* "—and he put a *lock and guards* on it so the **waters up above** wouldn't come falling down on us. And the other half he made into the ground way below."

> [!help] Grandfather
> Sah-Bah goes very still. He murmurs another word. "*Raqia.*" (that is the dome, the expanse). "*Mayim* above, *mayim* below." (that is the waters). His old eyes are shining a little. "And who, child, put the waters in their place in *our* telling? Was there a fight?"

> [!quote] All of us
> "No, Sah-Bah," *(more or less together)*. "Yahweh just made the dome and said where the waters go."

> [!help] Grandfather
> "Just so." *(Quiet.)* "Go on. There is more, I can see it on your faces."

> [!success] Child 03
> "There's more," *(heavily, as if he wishes there weren't)*. "After the fight, Marduk fixed everything up. He put the stars in their places, and he set up twelve months, and he hung the moon up to glow at night and mark the days, so everyone would always know what time it was."

> [!Info] Child 01
> "And that's the part that scares me most, Sah-Bah," *(barely above a whisper)*. "It sounds *like* ours. The lights — the *me'orot* — for the *moadim*, the appointed times, the days and the years. How are we even supposed to know which one is true, if they say almost the same things we do?"

> [!warning] Child 02
> "And from the sea-body he made the two big rivers pour out, and the mountains, and the springs of water."

> [!NOTE] Narrator
> Then they slow down. There is one more part, and it is the part that sits funny in the stomach.

> [!success] Child 03
> "And then they made *people*, Sah-Bah," *(slowly)*. "But… not nice. The gods were tired of working. So Marduk said, 'Let's make people to do all our work for us.' And they took the bad champion, Qingu, the one who started the war, and from his blood they made the very first humans. Just to be… workers. Slaves. So the gods could rest and never work again."

> [!NOTE] Narrator
> The room is quiet. Child 01 says the thing all of us are thinking.

> [!Info] Child 01
> "Sah-Bah… that's why they were laughing at us, I think. In the alley. They said *that's* what people are for. Working. Digging. Carrying. And our Abbas and Imas are out there right now, digging their canal in the sun…"

> [!help] Grandfather
> Sah-Bah reaches out and puts a heavy, gentle hand on the boy's scraggly head. He doesn't say anything for a moment. The eyebrow is down now, and his eyes are wet.
>
> "Tell me the rest, *yeled*. Finish their story." (that is child).

> [!warning] Child 02
> "There's almost no more," *(subdued)*. "Because the gods were so grateful, they built Marduk a giant city with a giant temple — Babylon, Sah-Bah, *this* place — so all the gods would have a house and a resting place. And everyone bowed and gave Marduk fifty grand names, because now he was the King forever and ever."

> [!success] Child 03
> "We've *seen* it," *(almost ashamed to admit it)*. "At their New Year, the big parade, when they carry their Marduk through the streets and everyone shouts. It's… it's very big, Sah-Bah."

> [!Info] Child 01
> *(And then Child 01 says it again, all of it this time, the thing we ran the whole way here to ask. His chin is wobbling.)* "Sah-Bah — is it *true*? Did their elohim really make everything? Is Yahweh even… is He real, if their god is so big? Are we just the people who *lost*?"

> [!help] Grandfather
> For a long moment Sah-Bah says nothing. His knees crackle as he leans forward on his stool, and he gathers us all in close with his eyes, every one of us.
>
> "So," he says at last, soft. "Their story. A world made out of a murdered mother. A sky that is a corpse. Gods who could not sleep, and could not stop fighting, and could not lift their own loads — so they made little people out of a traitor's blood to carry it for them. And a King who had to *win* the right to be God." He shakes his head, slow. "And they cornered you in an alley to tell you that *that* is what you are. A thing made to dig."

> [!Info] Child 01
> "…Yes, Sah-Bah." *(very small)*

> [!help] Grandfather
> "Banim." The old voice is steady now, and warm, and it fills the little room. "Listen to me. I am old, and I will tell you the truth, because I knew it before they ever put us on the road to this place. Their story is loud, and it is big. But it is *backwards*."

> [!help] Grandfather
> He holds up one crooked finger.
>
> "There were no two gods at the start. There was no war. There was no monster, and no murder. There was the One — *Elohim* — over the dark and the deep, over the *tehom*, His *Ruach* moved like a breath." (that is His Spirit, His wind).

> [!help] Grandfather
> "And He did not fight the waters, my children. He did not need to. He only… *spoke*. He said, 'Let there be light' — and there was light. And it was *good*. He spoke the dome, and set the *mayim* in their place, gentle as a father tucking a blanket. He hung the lights for the days and the seasons, not to win a war, but as a *gift*."

> [!help] Grandfather
> His old hand finds the boy's head again.
>
> "And when He made people — listen now, listen — He did not make you from a dead god's blood to carry His load. He made you in His own *tselem*. His image. His likeness." (that is image). "He breathed His own breath into the dust and called it His own, and *blessed* it, and said, 'Be fruitful. Rule. The whole garden is yours.' Not a slave, child. A son. A daughter. A little crown of the whole creation."

> [!help] Grandfather
> "And then He *rested* — not because He was tired, not because someone else took up His tools — but because the work was *finished*, and it was very good, and He made that seventh day holy and blessed it. *Shabbat*. A rest He gives to *you*, even here, even now, even in Babylon."

> [!NOTE] Narrator
> We are all very quiet. Outside, somewhere far off, we can hear the scrape of the canal-work going on in the heat.

> [!help] Grandfather
> Sah-Bah smiles then, and the bushy eyebrow lifts one last time, mischievous now.
>
> "So next time those big Babylonian boys corner you in the alley, you can tell them this. Their gods made a *world* and then needed slaves to run it. *Our* God made a world, and then made *us* to be free in it — and even now, He is coming to redeem us out of *theirs*."

> [!quote] All of us
> "…Will you tell us the whole thing, Sah-Bah? The real one? From the very beginning?"

> [!help] Grandfather
> The old man settles back, and his eyes go far away, and somewhere very deep he begins, the way his own Sah-Bah must have begun it, long ago, in a land none of us can remember:
> 
> "*Bereshit…*"

> [!quote] All of us
> *… bara elohim et ha-shamayim v-et ha-erets*
