# Righteousness and Anxiety 
> [!Info] Used
> - Parent’s Bible Study during Jan 2026

> [!NOTE] Related
> [[Sermon on the Mount Wealth]]


## Scripture
*“Therefore I tell you, do not worry about your life, what you will eat or drink, or about your body, what you will wear. Isn’t there more to life than food and more to the body than clothing? Look at the birds in the sky: They do not sow, or reap, or gather into barns, yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren’t you more valuable than they are? And which of you by worrying can add even one hour to his life? Why do you worry about clothing? Think about how the flowers of the field grow; they do not work or spin. Yet I tell you that not even Solomon in all his glory was clothed like one of these! And if this is how God clothes the wild grass, which is here today and tomorrow is tossed into the fire to heat the oven, won’t he clothe you even more, you people of little faith? So then, don’t worry saying, ‘What will we eat?’ or ‘What will we drink?’ or ‘What will we wear?’ For the unconverted pursue these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them. But above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well. So then, do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Today has enough trouble of its own.”* - **Matt 6:25-34**

All of **Matt 6:19-24** is why there is a “Therefore” statement! Because if you are truly a slave of God and a servant on the kingdom there will be anxiety! 
This is why we pray in **Matt 6:11**, “give us today our daily bread”. It’s not a metaphor, it’s a statement of being in need of the Father’s provision. 

This passage is quite interesting, it’s super straightforward if you just read it. But there is an interesting distinction that is made when you look at it carefully. The mention of *life* and *body*, is relevant. This we will unpack a bit. 
There is also very interesting kingdom language happening here.
It’s really about bearing the name of God rightly, much like the high priest with the plate on his forehead saying “Holiness to the LORD”.

It is difficult to apply this passage directly from the text to us in our modern world where there is no lack for us around the table, either of food or of clothing. But I posit that this does speak to a larger principle that is applicable to us still. 

## Structure
- **v25** is a command and the setup for the justification of it.
- **v26-27** is about the continuation of life, how that is in God’s hands. 
- **v28-30** is about bodily comfort and subjection to the elements. 
- **v31-33** picks up on the statement from **v25** and contrasts the two ways. 
- **v34** finalizes, summarizes and gives practical advice for the problem.

## Concepts

### Worry
It’s important that we understand what this word “worry” means. Otherwise we will be confused about this whole section.

Worry is innately future focused. It comes from being unsure of what lays ahead (Mammon). The context of what we are dealing with here is not about, but could apply to, things outside of continuation of life and lack of bodily protection.

> [!faq] BDAG (*merimnao*)
> Worry:
> - to be apprehensive, have anxiety, be anxious, be (unduly) concerned

There are only two other uses of this word (the lemma) outside of this passage in Matthew → **Matt 13:22; 10:19** 
It does show up in Luke → **Luke 21:29-38**
Also in a couple other places → **1 Cor 7:32-34; 1 Pet 5:5-11;**
And in the Septuagint → **1 Chron 17:9; LXX Ps 37:19**

### Body and Soul (more to life)
*Therefore I tell you, do not worry about your life, what you will eat or drink, or about your body, what you will wear. Isn’t there more to life than food and more to the body than clothing?* - **Matt 6:25**

It is interesting that the *life* (*psychē*) and *body* (*sōma*) are used here next to each other in a passage about anxiety around material provision. 

> [!faq] BDAG (*psyche*)
> Life:
> - the condition of being alive, earthly life, life itself
> 
> also 
> 
> - the soul is the center of both the earthly (1a) and the transcendent (2d) life

> [!faq] BDAG (*soma*)
> Body:
> - body of a human being or animal, body

They are also used together here → **Matt 10:26-33; 1 Thes 5:23-24**

While this passage is definitely focused around the actual life you are living now on earth, and your current earthly body. There is an echo of eternity in these words. *isn’t there MORE to life*, it’s a call to send your mind up to what is not perceivable. 
There is in fact more to life, there is a second one after it which you have to entirely entrust our Father for.
To worry on food and clothing, life and comfort so to lose focus on the kingdom and on God. → **Matt 13:22**

Interesting use of *psyche* → **2 Cor 12:15**

### Lessons from Nature (God’s providence)
*”Look at the birds in the sky: They do not sow, or reap, or gather into barns, yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren’t you more valuable than they are?”* - **Matt 6:26**

Jesus reasons with us here about why we should not be anxious. It’s an argument from lesser to greater (rabbinic technique), specifically using nature as an example.

Lessons from Nature:
- **Job 12:7–10; Prov 6:6–11; Ps 104:27-28; Jer 8:7** (**Jer 8:8** is really important but not relevant), **Psalm 127:1-2; Psalm 147:1-12;**

More valuable:
- **Matt 10:31; 12:12**
- **Gen 1:26–28; Gen 2:19–20**

It’s important to notice that the birds still work for their food, however God makes it readily available to them. They even had to do work for it when Yahweh literally made bread from heaven → **Ex 16:1-4**

> [!quote] Luther
> As Luther famously put it, God provides food for the birds, but he does not drop it into their beaks

Father provides us the means to sustain our lives, it’s rare that food just appears, but it has happened → **1 Kings 19:5-8** (even then the pre-incarnate Jesus had to do the work)

### Lifespan
*”And which of you by worrying can add even one hour to his life?”* - **Matt 6:27**

With the previous verses in mind, this statement is entirely true. Has the act of worrying or being anxious about food or anything else ever added time to your life. You’ll never know until it over and at that point it’s pointless.

> [!info] Fact
> - One hour of your life is *0.00014%* of the total hours you are likely to live (if you live until 79). 

If by worrying we can’t extend the very thing we cling to, then why worry.
Our life is *like a vapor* → **Ps 39:4-8**
Only God is in control of life and death, and the time between.
**2 Kings 20:1-6; Job 14:1-6; Prov 10:27; Deut 30:11-20**

### Flowers and Grass
*“Why do you worry about clothing? Think about how the flowers of the field grow; they do not work or spin. Yet I tell you that not even Solomon in all his glory was clothed like one of these! And if this is how God clothes the wild grass, which is here today and tomorrow is tossed into the fire to heat the oven, won’t he clothe you even more, you people of little faith?”* - **Matt 6:29-30**

There are some interesting things happening here, playing off the previous example of the birds. 

I would be hesitate to abstract this past clothing, into something like bodily security or well-being. While that is the theme is to do with bodily protect (clothing), it’s not promising a house or something more than “being clothed”. 
At the end of the day, to focus to much on the material aspects of these comparisons would be to miss the point entirely. 

It is interesting that for the birds, it *your heavenly Father* and for the grass it’s *God*. It’s missing that feeling on intimacy in the passage. 
Don’t know what to make of that, especially since all times grass and God are near each other in the Bible, it’s always God or Yahweh (the LORD) → **Job 40:15; Ps 104:14; Ps 106:20; Isa 40:7; Mic 5:7; Matt 6:30; Luke 12:28** 
#### Action
In contrast to the birds, the grass and flowers does nothing. Plants are entirely passive in their growth, they don’t sow or reap, work or spin, they simply exist, doing what God intended them too. 

Not like Solomon, who was clothed by human hands, that toiled an spun. 

#### Lifespan
The is direct mention of the lifespan of the wild grass, or the grass of the fields. There was no mention of lifespan for tweety bird. Grass is *here today, and gone tomorrow*. It’s fleeting, much like humanity, which is probably why it’s used in reference to us so often → **Ps 103:15-18; Isa 40:6-8; Job 8:12**
It once again reminds us to look towards God for reliance for everything.

#### Ecosystem
There is a fun little ecosystem happening in that passage. 
God, provides food for the birds. The birds provide food for us.
The grass and flowers are “dressed” by God, for them to be used for our ovens to cook the birds. (particular oven being referred to is action a bread oven)
God already provides our food for us, all we do worry between meals. 

### Little Faith
*. . . you people of little faith?”* - **Matt 6:30**

Little faith must not be confused for no faith, that is very clearly delineated by the Greek.

Other uses of “little faith”, it’s used six times in total in the Bible, five in Matthew and one in Luke. The Luke verse is the mirror of **Matt 6:30**
**Matt 8:23-26; 14:22-32; 16:5-12; 17:14-19**
In the other gospels it’s practically akin to unbelief.

The disciples journey of little faith, well worth a study. There might be a key to understanding it symbolically with their environments when it’s mentioned. Mountain → Storm with Jesus → Storm, but taking action but doubting → Land, but doubting → next? 

> [!faq] BDAG 1.(*oligopistos*) 2.(*oligopistia*)
> Little Faith:
> 1. of little faith/trust, only in the synoptics and only in addressing the disciples
> 2. littleness/poverty of faith → **Matt 17:20**

Compared with:

> [!faq] BDAG (*apistia*)
> Unbelief: 
> - unwillingness to commit oneself to another or respond positively to the other’s words or actions, lack of belief, unbelief

Little faith is faith in Jesus’ humanity, but not in his deity. Or in God’s existence, but not his actuality.
It’s faith that can’t move our from acknowledgement into action, there is no trust in God for anything other than salvation and barely that. 

> [!quote] R.T. France
> In **17:20** we shall see that *oligopistia* means having faith less than a mustard seed, in effect no faith at all. “Faith,” in Matthew, means the confidence that God can and will act on his people’s behalf; without that, however much a person may “believe” intellectually, they are for practical purposes “faithless.”

Faith is actualized through action, not only in the “heart”. 
We wouldn’t have reason to believe the Bible if Jesus had simply stayed at home, doing carpentry and then died of old again. We know Jesus had faith by physical action he took to implement the Father’s will on earth. It’s not that works save, but they are the outworking of continued faith, that without them your faith is dead → **James 2:14–26**

> [!quote] Eliezer the Great (Rabbi)
> “Whoever has a piece of bread in his basket and says, ‘What shall I eat tomorrow?’ belongs only to them who are little in faith.”

### Seeking Kingdoms
*“So then, don’t worry saying, ‘What will we eat?’ or ‘What will we drink?’ or ‘What will we wear?’ For the unconverted pursue these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them. But above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well.”* - **Matt 6:31-33**

#### The Two Kingdoms
What we are looking at here is kingdom language, the juxtaposition of the *unconverted (Gentiles)* and *his kingdom*, is meant to lead your mind to think in these terms. Why and why does it matter, yes we are in God’s kingdom, but we are also on earth, so technically we are physically in the same as the “Gentiles”. 
Let’s break this down so we can know what it matters. 


> [!faq] BDAG (*ethnos*)
> Unconverted (Gentiles):
> - a body of persons united by kinship, culture, and common traditions, nation, people
> 
> or in **Matt 6:32**
> 
> - those who do not belong to groups professing faith in the God of Israel, the nations, gentiles, unbelievers (in effect=‘polytheists’)


> [!faq] BDAG (*basileia*)
> His Kingdom:
> - of God’s rule *the royal reign* of God

We are getting language here around those ruled by God, and those not ruled by him. Those by anything other than God! In fact they don’t even acknowledge that they should, live by any standard greater that what they define or feel. → **Eph 2:11-21; 4:17-24; 1 Pet 4:1-6**

This is especially interesting when you think about this while reading of Gentiles being praise for their faith → **Matt 8:5-13; 15:21-28**

So whats the problem, really with anxiety, worrying?
It’s that you look and act exactly like those that don’t know God! Nothing quite tells the world that you have an All Powerful God and Father in heaven like worrying about whats for lunch!

We cannot be *the light of the world* or *give light to the whole house*. If what we show to the world is darkness. We cannot… we MUST not… be like the world, how else are they going to wonder about *the hope that we possess*. 
We have to discard our worries around our life and our provision and instead seek God’s kingship, his kingdom and his righteousness as a matter of utmost priority.

We don’t sit there anxiously wondering if our parents are going to make food for us when they invite us for dinner. Why should we treat our provision from God any different.

#### Seeking and Righteousness
*“But above all pursue his kingdom and righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well.”* - **Matt 6:33**
We are told to pursue (seek in other translations), God’s kingdom and his righteousness. This is especially interesting because it plays off the pursue (seeking) that the Gentiles do for earthly things.  


> [!faq] BDAG (*epizeteo*)
> Pursue (gentiles)
> - to be seriously interested in or have a strong desire for

It’s interesting that even the word used to describe the unbeliever’s pursuing things, is a strong compound word (meaning that it’s made up of two words)


> [!faq] BDAG (*zeteo*)
> Pursue (believers)
> - to devote serious effort to realize one’s desire or objective, strive for, aim (at), try to obtain, desire, wish (for)

Even the word used for believers is singularly focused, without any compounds.

We must also consider the word righteousness, it has a constellation of meanings in the but Hebrew and Greek. To lump all meanings into one is to miss the full picture of what “righteousness” entails. 

> [!faq] BDAG (*dikaiosynē*)
> Righteousness:
> - the quality or characteristic of upright behavior, uprightness, righteousness
> 
> specifically in **Matt 6:33**:
> 
> - of the kind of δ. God expects (on δ. as characteristic required by God acc. to Jewish perspective)

Righteousness is to be:
- *fulfilled* → **Matt 3:15**
- *hungered and thirsted after* → **Matt 5:6**
- it will be *persecuted* → **Matt 5:10**
- it can be *exceeded* → **Matt 5:20**
- it can be *displayed* → **Matt 6:1**
- it can be *pursued* and it is something is *God’s* → **Matt 6:33**
- it’s a *way* walked that the religious won’t believe → **Matt 21:32**

> [!quote] Benno Przybylski
> in Matthew: “Righteousness is seen as God’s demand upon man. Righteousness refers to proper conduct before God.”

It’s not just about avoiding what is said to be bad and doing what is said to be good. Righteousness is understanding the principles behind the law, the “heart” of the law and seeking having your morality conformed to God’s standard → **Matt 5:21–48**

As members of God’s kingdom (which is your life, your being), under his ruler-ship, we are expected to seek to meet God’s righteous standard → **Matt 5:48**
It’s not about getting into the kingdom, for that is by faith in Christ alone → **Gal 3:6-13; Heb 11:6-19**
But it is about demonstrating your faith by acting in accordance with God’s standards, his will and his control → **James 2:14-25**
Which is will fall short of, but that is why we are justified by faith alone. The point is to desire and wish for it.

#### All Things
If we seek our Father’s kingdom as a matter or priority, Jesus has told us that *“. . . all these things will be given to you as well.”* - **Matt 6:33**. This isn’t referring all luxuries, nice things or even good food. God will give us all that is required for survival. 
This phrase is in what is called a *divine passive*. This is where the the verb (the doing word) is passive (meaning you aren’t doing anything) and God is the one acting. 
This is why we pray *give us our daily bread* and trust that our Father will provide.

### Trouble for Each Day
*“So then, do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Today has enough trouble of its own.”* - **Matt 6:34**

This is not talking about eating and drinking and living it up because God will provide more. It’s much rather focusing on just doing everything you can today without being paralyzed by worry for what is to come. Each day will have it’s troubles, so simply keep focusing on doing what is set before you today. Wringing out each day of the opportunities for righteousness it can provide. 

Jesus is the perfect example of this, despite his full knowledge of what he must suffer. He wasn’t ever anxious until it was clearly his time.

He did not stress about this time, until it was literally the day of this crucifixion. 
**Matt 26:31; 36-45; 27:1, 45, 57**
Note → Jesus was strengthened during his prayer, which allowed him to pray more earnestly, allowing him to finish his prayer and have his distress relieved. **Luke 22:43**

> [!faq] BDAG (*ademoneo*)
> Distressed:
> - be in anxiety, be distressed, troubled

We pray to our Father about our anxieties and worries, then trust that he will provide for us → **Matt 6:11, 32; Heb 13:5-8; Phil 4:6–7;**
