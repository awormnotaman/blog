# Introduction

We must remember that the Sermon on the Mount is being taught:
1. To Jews
2. To his disciples (**Matthew 5:1**), the crowds were not the intended audience.
3. To uncover and overturn the Pharisaical oral law **Matthew 5:20**
4. To contrast two ways, their understanding of righteousness (works) and God’s intention our righteousness (a demonstration of faith, counter to the world’s ways). **Matthew 5:21-22**
The section we are covering is among the section of the Sermon that is about what the Practice of true righteousness is.
At the core of Jesus’s teaching in this portion of our relationship to wealth, possessions and centrally, what we do for security in this world.

I’ve done my best to honor the Lord by keeping this practical and not overly theological, which is in with keeping with the spirit of the Sermon.

# Righteousness and Treasure 

## Scripture
*“Do not accumulate for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and devouring insect destroy and where thieves break in and steal. But accumulate for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moth and devouring insect do not destroy, and thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also. 
The eye is the lamp of the body. If then your eye is healthy, your whole body will be full of light. But if your eye is diseased, your whole body will be full of darkness. If then the light in you is darkness, how great is the darkness! 
No one can serve two masters, for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money.”* - **Matthew 6:19–24 (NET 2nd ed.)**

## Structure
- In **v19-20** the focus is corporate, referring to everyone in plurals *“yourselves”*. Jesus is addressing everyone! Everyone can either have treasure in heaven, or treasure on earth.
- In **v21-24** the focus shifts to you, individually, with *“your”* and *“you”*. The understanding of where your treasures are and what your attitude towards them are. It’s also your responsibility to change it! You are the one who must choose.
- **v21**, tells you the problem with treasures on earth. 
- **v22-23**, contrasts and defines the problem in actual actionable terms.
- **v24**, raises the standard and uncovers the true radical nature of this issue where your treasure is. It’s the “who do you choose” moment. 

> [!INFO] Scripture
> Progression of the text is interesting.
> There is a progression from **heart** to **eye** to **slave**. 
> What is in your heart is what your eyes look at, what your eyes look at will make you it’s slave. 

## Concepts
### Treasures on Earth vs Heaven
*“Do not accumulate for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and devouring insect destroy and where thieves break in and steal. But accumulate for yourselves treasures in heaven, where moth and devouring insect do not destroy, and thieves do not break in and steal.”* - **Matt 6:19-20**

There is a theme in the Sermon on the Mount of *rewards in heaven* opposed to *rewards on earth*. 
On earth → **Matthew 5:46; 6:1; 6:2; 6:5; 6:16**
In heaven → **Matthew 5:12; 6:4; 6:6; 6:18**

We have just come out of a previous section of scripture which ends with *“And your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you.”*

But we are now in versus **19-24** tackling something different. The language has shifted from “rewards” to “treasures”. We are looking at our attitude towards these rewards whether on earth or in heaven and weighing up their value. 
#### Earthly
We are told what earthly treasures are by defining three categories, **v19**.
1. They are on earth
2. They can be destroyed
3. They can be stolen
Scripture’s attitude to these treasures →  **Psalm 39:1-13; James 5:1-6; Luke 12:13-21; Psalm 49**
#### Heavenly
We are also told what heavenly treasures are by those same categories, **v20**.
1. They are in heaven
2. They cannot be destroyed
3. They cannot be stolen
Scripture’s attitude to these treasures →  **Matthew 19:16-22, 1 Tim 6:17-19**

> [!faq] BDAG (*thesauros*)
> Treasures:
> - that which is stored up, treasure

It’s telling how little the NT mentions treasures in heaven. There is tons of talk of being rewarded for good works, but little about treasures. If rewards if is something that contributes to a treasure, we are really being told just do the next good thing, there is a treasure waiting. This is emphasized in **v22**. 

### Heart
*”For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.”* - **Matt 6:21**
Now we hit **v21**, where the focus shifts, with our first *“your”* statement. Indicating that what we are talking about is *“your”* responsibility, not the Body’s or God’s.

> [!faq] BDAG (*kardia*)
>    Heart: 
>    - as center and source of the whole inner life, w. its thinking feeling, and volition.
>    

In **v21** - of the emotions, wishes, desires
Other uses of the same work → **Mt 5:28; 12:34f; Lk 6:45; 12:34; 24:32; Js 3:14; 5:8**. 

The first usage of “heart” in the entirety of Matthew, is in **Matt 5:8**
The first use of “heart” in the Bible → **Gen 6:5-6** (interesting how it’s negative)
The very intentions of their minds were only evil. Yahweh was “cut to the heart”, the very center of his being was offended (embarrassed/insulted).

This is one of the keys (probably the) to bring context to what we are reading in the Sermon. So let’s see what scriptures says about the heart.
#### Ouch Scriptures
- **Deut 8:17; 17:17**
- **Matthew 12:34; 15:18–19; 22:37**
The heart is the seat of:
- *lust* (**Mt 5:28**)
- *disobedience* (**Mt 24:48**)
- *obtuseness* (**Mk 8:17**)
- *doubt* (**Mk 11:23; Lk 24:38**)
- *malicious plans* (**Lk 12:45**)
- *fear* (**Jn 14:1**)
- *drunkenness* and *anxiety* (**Lk 21:34**)
- *Satanic attack* (**Mt 13:19; Jn 13:2**)
- *evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts and defiling words* (**Mt 15:18–19; Mk 7:21**)
#### Amen Scriptures
- **Matthew 5:8; 11:29**
The heart is the place of: 
- *rejoicing* (**Jn 16:22**)
- *purity* (**Mt 5:8**)
- *worship* (**Mt 15:8**)
- *forgiveness* (**Mt 18:35**)
- *the love of God* (**Mt 22:37**)
- *repentance* (**Lk 1:17**) 
- *spiritual awakening* upon encountering the risen Christ (**Luke 24:32**) 
#### Summery
In sum, the heart represents the human will, where:
- *inner thoughts conceive* (**Mt 9:4; Lk 1:51; 2:35; 9:47**)
- *faith flourishes or dies* (**Lk 8:12**)
- *treasure up objects of worship* (**Mt 6:21; Lk 12:34**)

The concept of the heart (*kardia*) in the Gospels carries over from the OT term (*lēb*, *lēbab*), which refers to “all aspects of a person: vital, emotive, noetic, and voluntative”.

It is a concept that is way to broad to go into properly now. But the important thing to note is that the “heart” is more than just the fuzzy feeling in our chests, or the thing that aches when we are grieving. It’s the whole shebang.


### Eyes are the Lamp
*“The eye is the lamp of the body. If then your eye is healthy, your whole body will be full of light. But if your eye is diseased, your whole body will be full of darkness. If then the light in you is darkness, how great is the darkness!”* - **Matt 6:22-23**

This portion of scripture is adding weight to two pieces of scripture we  have touched on already. **Matt 5:13-16; 6:1-4**

We are dealing with a fascinating piece of wordplay here that the English just can’t properly convey. Along with that is a knowledge of a Second Temple Jewish saying and an antiquated worldview, to fully grasp this.

Jesus is not teaching “the eye is the window of the soul”, which is a modern perspective on this text. 

#### First → The Worldview
In antiquity, people believed that your eyes contained light, which shined out of them into reveal what is in the world. This is were we get all our sayings in our Bibles about “light of the eyes”. **Prov 15:30**
This is why poor eyesight is described as having “dim” eyes. **Gen 27:1** 
It’s also why Jesus’s eyes are described as “blazing torches” and as “seven lamps” (signifying his omniscience). **Dan 10:3-6; Zech 4:2-10** 

Strong, bright eyes illuminate and discern all, and they are good indicator of inner well being. 
A bad eye, by contrast, sees double and is blurred.

#### Secondly → The Words Used
The Greek word for “healthy” is *haplous*, it actually means “single” or “sincere”.

> [!FAQ] BDAG (*haplous*)
> - to being motivated by singleness of purpose so as to be open and aboveboard, single, without guile, sincere, straightforward i.e. without a hidden agenda

The Greek word for “diseased” is *ponēros*, it actually means “evil” or “wicked”.

> [!FAQ] BDAG (*ponēros*)
> - to being morally or socially worthless, wicked, evil, bad, base, worthless, vicious, degenerate

The issue with these two words, is that they aren’t natural opposites. Like they kinda are, but not really. This is where the derivative noun of *haplous* steps in, *haplotēs*.

The Greek word *haplotēs* means “sincerity” or “generosity”.

> [!FAQ] BDAG (*haplotēs*)
> - Of simple goodness, which gives itself without reserve, ‘without strings attached’, ‘without hidden agendas’, ingenuousness → (innocent and unsuspecting)

Which is a natural opposite of *ponēros*, in the context of wealth and it’s uses.

#### Thirdly → The Evil vs Good Eye
There was a Hebrew idiom around the “sincere” eye vs the “evil” eye.
The sincere or good eye was linked to “generosity”, the one that possesses a good eye is generous with their acts and thoughts. They were said to be on the “good way”.
The evil eye was linked to “niggardliness” (to be ungenerous or mean), the one with an evil eye was ungenerous and gave grudgingly. They were said to be on the “evil way”.
Out of the abundance of light or darkness within someone their eyes would look at people and things that would bring goodness or harm to those looked at. 

#### Summing It Up
The eye reveals the overflow of the heart. If your eyes are singularly focused on what is to come, you will interact in the world with generosity, this is the outworking of the light within you.
But if the overflow of your heart is evil, ungenerous and grudging, then the light you give to the world is darkness. If the very actions of light within you are done out of darkness, how great is that darkness!
*“No one can serve two masters, for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money.”*

This wording is used in **Matt 20:15** in the parable of the Workers in the Vineyard. 
The second part of that verse in the Greek is *“Am I not permitted to do what I want with what belongs to me? Is your eye evil because I am generous?’”*

There are several other places were this concept appears, which I haven’t had time to unpack.
**LXX Deut 15:9**, **Prov 22:9; 23:6; 28:22**, **KJV/NKJV Mark 7:22**


### Two Masters
*“No one can serve two masters, for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money.”* - **Matt 6:24**
Now we hit the crux of what we have been reading. The “who do you choose” statement. This really does just mean what is says, but there are a couple points worth expanding upon.
The fact that Jesus, in one verse says two love/hate, statements means he really means what he says and is doing his best to frame the concept in our minds.

#### Serving
The word for *“serve”* is understated, it has the full force of the actions of a slave serving their master. 

> [!faq] BDAG (*douleuo*)
> Serve:
> - to act or conduct oneself as one in total service to another, perform the duties of a slave, serve, obey

It is no small thing that this terminology is used here in this Sermon. The Israelite are nation born out of a perpetual slavery. They are often reminded in the OT. They were supposed to only serve God as their LORD and master. But yet they kept binding themselves to be slaves to the gods of the nations, idols, laws and willingly returning themselves from God’s freedom to slavery. Their entire nations history is foreshadowing were we are right now.

#### Despise
Another note, the word for “despise” can be translated “contempt”. 

> [!faq] BDAG (*kataphroneo*)
>   Despise:
>   - to look down on someone or someth. with contempt or aversion, with implication that one considers the object of little value, look down on, despise, scorn, treat with contempt

> [!faq] Oxford Dictionary (*contempt*)
> Contempt:
> - the feeling that someone or something is worthless or beneath consideration.

Do we ever simply not consider how God would have us use our wealth? Or do we submit what we have to God’s usage and for displaying the light of the kingdom? 

### Money/Mammon
The quick obligatory note on the word “money”/Mammon, which is every preacher’s favorite word to expound on.

> [!TIP] Mammon
> Mammon is an Aramaic word that means *“firm”*, *“sure”* or *“certain”*. It is therefor anything that you place reliance on or assurance of the future in. 
> It refers to wealth and material security in all it’s forms. Not just cold hard cash.
