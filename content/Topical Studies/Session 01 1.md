# Introductory Points
## About the Epistle
- Written against syncretism (the blending of two  different beliefs and practices) 
- It covers Christian beliefs and behaviors 
- The theme is really similar Colossians, suggesting that it was written at the same time. There is a high likelihood that the churches in the area of Colosse, Laodicea, Hierapolis, and Ephesus were struggling with the same issues.
- The letter focuses on basics of the Christian belief and behavior. It doesn't address specific concerns like false teachings, which Paul often deals with.  

## The City (Ephesus)
- One of the largest trade hubs of it's era. 
- It was a melting pot of cultures.
- It had the largest theater in the historic world, sat 24,000 people.
- Was the home of the Temple of Diana (Artemis). 
- Think of it almost like a ancient Hollywood.

## The Church
- Is an important site in biblical history.
- Many great letters were written to and from Ephesus, we are going to read one.
	- 1 and 2 Timothy
	- One of the letters in Revelations is to Ephesus
	- The Gospel and letters of John were written in Ephesus
- Paul was so effective there when he visited in Acts 18-20, that it crippled the Diana idol trade and the silversmiths tried to have him killed.

## Letter Structure
This is the most systematic letter in the Bible. It is regarded as Paul's finest writing. 
The first half (Chapters 1-3) deals with our relationship to God through Christ.
The second half (Chapters 4-6) deals with our relationship to others in the Lord. 
Paul uses the title *Christ* when referring to our relationship to God and *Lord* when referring to our relationship to each other, which is a detail worthwhile pondering on.

In the first half Paul outlines how salvation comes to believers and in the second half how we should behave after having become believers. We are saved *for* good works **not by** good works. 

## Hot Topics 

### Predestination
A topic not to be confused about, we are predestined to holiness and good works. Not to salvation, see points below.
There are two major viewpoints on this, but only one is biblical. 
Note, there are many fantastic Christian that hold to either doctrine. It doesn’t change your actual salvation, but it can lead to having to twist scripture to fit it into doctrine. 

>[!multi-column]
>> [!warning] Calvin *(Calvinism)*
>> - predestined to salvation
>> - predestined per individual
>> - persons are predestined
>> - irresistible, the person **will** believe because they are predestined
>> - saved only by grace
>> - destiny determined by God's choice
>> - those that are lost - are not chosen by God to believe 
>> - born again before repentance and faith, because God destined it
>> - perseverance guaranteed, you will never fall away, because God destined it
>
>> [!success] Arminus *(Arminianism)*
>> - predestined to service
>> - predestined corporately, the Body of Christ
>> - a people, the born again are predestined
>> - conditional, destiny is the decision of the individual
>> - saved by one’s faith
>> - destiny dependent on our choice
>> - those that are lost - are lost because of their choice
>> - born again after repentance and faith
>> - perseverance required, because belief is a choice, one must continue to believe.


### Salvation *(Once Saved, Always Saved?)*
Salvation is a process, not a singular event. To be saved one must run their race in faithfulness to Jesus and to God the Father. 
(Some scriptures, **Matthew 24:13**, **Romans 5:10**, **1 Corinthians 5:5**, **1 Timothy 4:16**, **Hebrews 9:28**)
